### [0:00 - 0:20] THE HOOK (Camera: Physical Phone in Hand)
"Look, we all have that friend—or honestly, maybe it’s you—where it's 4 PM, you've survived entirely on iced coffee and sheer anxiety, and you suddenly realize: 'shit, I haven't had a single drop of water today.'

Every hydration app tries to fix this with push notifications. But what do we actually do? We swipe them away in half a second and forget about them. 

So I thought: what if your phone just physically drowned the longer you ignored it? 

That’s why we built Seequid. The name is literally 'See-Liquid'—because you actually see liquid flooding your screen."

---

### [0:20 - 0:45] THE OVERLAY & TOUCH PROOF (Screen: scrcpy Recording)
"Here’s how it works. As you fall behind on your daily water pace, water slowly creeps up from the bottom of your screen over whatever app you're using. 

Now, the obvious question: 'Wait, doesn’t that block my taps?' 

Nope. We built this with Android's system overlay APIs, capped the window opacity at 75%, and made it strictly non-touchable. You can still scroll Twitter, type on your keyboard, and send texts right through the water. It’s an ambient signal that stays on your screen without getting in your way.

When the tide gets high, our little squid buddy docks right on the edge. You tap him, pick how much you just drank—like a quick 250 ml glass—and boom, the water immediately drains away."

---

### [0:45 - 1:05] INSIDE THE APP & THE ENGINE (Screen: App Dashboard)
"Inside the app, everything is built natively with Kotlin and Jetpack Compose. 

We don't do dumb arbitrary timers. Hydration pacing is calculated as a pure mathematical function based on your waking hours and the clock. So even if your phone dies or you restart it, your water level stays 100% accurate with zero phantom backlog.

And if you want to test it out, we have Demo Mode right here on the home screen, which squeezes an entire day's pace into 10 minutes so you can watch the wave physics do their thing."
